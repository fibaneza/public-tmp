== Why RAG Needs Its Own Answer

Ordinary service monitoring assumes failure is observable. A broken web service
returns a 500; a saturated queue grows; a crashed process stops responding.

RAG breaks that assumption in three specific ways:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Property |Consequence for instrumentation

|*Failure is a 200 OK*
|A pipeline retrieving the wrong chunks returns a fluent, confident, well-formed answer. Error rate, latency and saturation are all nominal. Nothing you would normally alarm on moves.

|*There is no ground truth in production*
|Offline you have labelled queries and can compute recall. Online you have neither, so every quality signal is a *proxy*, and you must know which proxy measures what.

|*The pipeline is a funnel, not a chain*
|1M chunks → 50 candidates → 5 chunks → 1 answer. A regression can be at any narrowing, and an aggregate latency or error metric cannot tell you which.
|===

So RAG observability is mostly about *building proxies for quality* and
*attributing an outcome to a stage*. Everything else is ordinary service
monitoring.

== The RAG Trace

One trace per query, one span per stage. This is the single highest-value
artefact — it is what makes
xref:RAG_Operations.adoc#_the_debugging_decision_tree[the debugging decision
tree] mechanical rather than guesswork.

=== Span Attributes That Earn Their Place

A span with only a duration tells you where time went and nothing about why the
answer was wrong. These are the attributes that make a trace diagnostic:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Span |Attributes

|`rag.query` (root)
|`query.text_hash`, `query.token_count`, `session.id`, `user.id`, `tenant.id`, `pipeline.version`, `index.snapshot`

|`rag.contextualize`
|`was_rewritten` (bool), `history.turns` — the bool is what lets you split metrics by follow-up vs first turn

|`rag.search.*`
|`retriever.type`, `k`, `results.count`, `score.top`, `score.min`, `score.mean`, `filter.applied`

|`rag.fuse`
|`inputs.count`, `overlap.count` — how many chunks both retrievers found, a direct signal of whether hybrid is earning its keep

|`rag.rerank`
|`candidates.count`, `top_n`, `score.top`, `rank_change.max`, `truncated` (bool)

|`rag.compress`
|`chars.before`, `chars.after`, `chunks.dropped`

|`rag.generate`
|`model`, `tokens.input`, `tokens.output`, `tokens.cached`, `cost_micro_usd`, `ttft_ms`, `finish_reason`

|`rag.verify_citations`
|`claims.total`, `claims.verified`, `claims.failed`
|===

*The one non-negotiable: record the retrieved chunk ids at every stage* —
candidates, post-rerank, and final. Ids, not text: text is a copy of your corpus
landing in a trace store with different access rules and retention, and you can
resolve ids on demand through the same ACL check as retrieval.

[source,python]
----
with tracer.start_as_current_span("rag.rerank") as span:
    reranked = reranker.rerank(query, candidates, top_n=5)
    span.set_attribute("rag.rerank.candidates_count", len(candidates))
    span.set_attribute("rag.rerank.score_top", float(reranked[0].score))
    # Ids only — never the chunk text.
    span.set_attribute("rag.rerank.result_ids", [c.id for c, _ in reranked])
    # Did reranking actually change anything? A max rank change of 0 across
    # many queries means the stage is paying latency for nothing.
    span.set_attribute("rag.rerank.rank_change_max",
                       max_rank_delta(candidates, reranked))
----

=== OpenTelemetry GenAI Conventions

Use the standard `gen_ai.*` attribute names where they exist rather than
inventing your own — vendor dashboards and integrations are built against them:

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
|Standard attribute |Carries

|`gen_ai.system`
|The provider

|`gen_ai.request.model` / `gen_ai.response.model`
|Requested vs actually served — these differ under fallback routing

|`gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` / `gen_ai.usage.output_tokens`
|Token counts

|`gen_ai.response.finish_reasons`
|`end_turn`, `max_tokens`, `refusal` — a rising `max_tokens` rate means truncated answers
|===

Retrieval has no equally settled convention, so namespace your own consistently
under `rag.*` and document it once.

== Quality Proxies

You have no ground truth in production. These are the signals that correlate
with quality, and — importantly — what each one actually measures.

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Signal |Rising means |Watch out

|*Retrieval score distribution* (top-1, mean of top-k)
|Falling = queries drifting away from what the corpus covers, or an index problem
|Scores are not comparable across embedding models. A step change at a deploy is the model, not the world

|*Score gap* (top-1 minus top-k)
|A narrow gap means nothing stood out — the retriever is guessing
|Naturally narrow on broad questions; segment by query type

|*Refusal rate*
|Up: retrieval degraded. *Down: worse* — the model started answering ungrounded
|The only signal here where both directions are bad

|*Citation verification failure rate*
|The model is asserting things its sources do not support
|The earliest hallucination signal you can compute mechanically. See xref:RAG_Citations.adoc#_quote_verification[quote verification]

|*Context utilisation* (chunks cited ÷ chunks supplied)
|Low = you are paying for context the model ignores; `k` is too high
|Not a quality metric on its own — a low value with good answers means you can cut cost

|*"No results" rate*
|A filter or index broke, or a whole source stopped ingesting
|Distinguish "zero hits" from "hits below threshold" — different causes

|*Follow-up / rephrase rate*
|Users immediately rephrasing means the first answer missed
|Strongest real user signal available without asking them

|*Answer length distribution*
|A sudden shift usually means a prompt or model change, not a corpus change
|Cheap canary for unannounced provider model updates
|===

*Refusal rate deserves the emphasis.* A RAG system that stops saying "I don't
know" has not become smarter — it has started answering from model knowledge
instead of your corpus, which is precisely the failure RAG exists to prevent.
Alert on movement in *either* direction.

== Per-Stage Metrics

Low cardinality, safe to alarm on. High-cardinality context (user, session,
query, chunk ids) belongs in the trace and the log —
xref:../../Tech-questions/Monitoring_Traceability_Alerts.adoc#_cardinality_economics[the cardinality rule]
applies unchanged here.

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Stage |Metrics |Labels (bounded only)

|Ingestion
|`documents_indexed`, `chunks_created`, `parse_failures`, `dlq_depth`, `index_lag_seconds`
|`source`, `doc_type`, `status`

|Retrieval
|`retrieval_latency`, `results_returned`, `score_top`, `zero_results`
|`retriever` (dense/sparse/hybrid), `filtered` (bool)

|Reranking
|`rerank_latency`, `candidates_in`, `rank_change_max`, `truncated_inputs`
|`model`

|Context
|`context_tokens`, `chunks_supplied`, `compression_ratio`
|`compressed` (bool)

|Generation
|`generation_latency`, `ttft`, `tokens_in/out/cached`, `cost_micro_usd`
|`model`, `finish_reason`, `status`

|Citations
|`claims_total`, `claims_verified`, `verification_failures`
|`status`

|End to end
|`query_latency`, `refusals`, `queries`
|`route`, `status`, `is_followup`
|===

Two derived metrics worth computing explicitly because they answer questions the
raw numbers do not:

[source,python]
----
# Your own overhead, isolated from provider time. This is the number that says
# whether to optimise your code or change provider.
overhead_ms = query_latency_ms - generation_latency_ms

# Is reranking earning its latency? If rank_change_max is ~0 across a large
# sample, the stage is pure cost on this corpus.
rerank_value = mean(rank_change_max)
----

`truncated_inputs` is the sleeper. A cross-encoder silently scores only the
first 512 tokens of a longer chunk, so a parent-expansion change can quietly
disable reranking with no error anywhere. Count it and alarm on it.

== Ingestion Is Half the System

The worst failure in RAG is a document that *silently* failed to index: the
system answers "I don't have information about that", and everyone blames the
model.

[mermaid]
....
%%{init: {"flowchart": {"nodeSpacing": 30, "rankSpacing": 38, "padding": 8}} }%%
flowchart LR
    S[Source of truth] --> R{{"Reconciliation<br/>diff (doc_id, content_hash)"}}
    I[(Index)] --> R
    R --> M["<b>missing</b><br/>never ingested<br/>→ alert"]
    R --> ST["<b>stale</b><br/>changed, not re-indexed<br/>→ alert"]
    R --> O["<b>orphaned</b><br/>deleted at source,<br/>still answering<br/>→ SECURITY alert"]

    style O fill:#fce8e6,stroke:#ea4335
    style M fill:#fef7e0,stroke:#fbbc04
....

Nothing in the *query* path can detect any of these — the index looks healthy
because it is internally consistent. A scheduled reconciliation is the only
thing that finds them; mechanics are in
xref:RAG_Operations.adoc#_drift[Drift].

Also instrument `index_lag_seconds` — the age of the oldest un-indexed change.
It is the SLI behind a freshness SLO, and the one users complain about as "it
doesn't know about the new policy".

== RAG-Specific Alerts

Ordinary alerts (latency, error rate, saturation) apply unchanged. These are the
ones that exist *because* RAG fails silently:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Alert |Condition |Means

|*Refusal rate shift*
|Step change either direction
|Retrieval degraded, or the model started answering ungrounded

|*Citation verification failures*
|Above baseline
|Drifting toward unsupported claims — the earliest mechanical hallucination signal

|*Zero-results rate*
|Step change
|Index or filter broke; or a source stopped ingesting

|*Retrieval score collapse*
|Mean top-1 drops sharply
|Wrong embedding model deployed, index corruption, or a re-index that did not complete

|*Reconciliation delta*
|`missing`/`stale` over threshold
|Ingestion is broken and the corpus is going stale invisibly

|*Reconciliation orphaned*
|Any, immediately
|Deleted documents still answering — treat as a security issue

|*Rerank truncation*
|`truncated_inputs` > 0
|Chunks exceed the cross-encoder window; reranking is scoring fragments

|*Index lag*
|Above the freshness SLO
|Ingestion pipeline stalled

|*Cost per query*
|Above budget
|Usually an agentic path firing more often than expected
|===

Two RAG-shaped SLOs worth defining, in the split from
xref:RAG_Operations.adoc#_slos_for_a_probabilistic_system[SLOs for a
probabilistic system]:

* *Groundedness* — ≥98% of claims pass citation verification. Mechanical, cheap,
  computable in real time, and the closest thing to a quality SLO you can page on.
* *Freshness* — 99% of source changes searchable within N minutes. Measured by
  reconciliation, not by hope.

Aggregate answer *quality* is a release gate, not a pager: LLM-judged metrics
have run-to-run variance, and alerting on them produces a noisy pager the team
learns to ignore.

== Sampling

Standard sampling reasoning applies, with one RAG-specific inversion.

The traces you most want are the ones where retrieval scored *badly* — and those
are exactly the queries a uniform sample discards. Force retention on:

[source,python]
----
def should_keep_trace(trace) -> bool:
    return (
        trace.error                              # always
        or trace.refused                         # "I don't know" — was that right?
        or trace.citation_failures > 0           # possible hallucination
        or trace.retrieval_score_top < THRESHOLD # the interesting failure mode
        or trace.zero_results
        or trace.latency_ms > SLO_MS
        or trace.flagged_by_user                 # thumbs-down
        or random.random() < BASELINE_RATE       # baseline for comparison
    )
----

Keep the baseline sample — without a healthy population to compare against, you
cannot tell whether a bad-looking trace is unusual.

== Instrumentation Order

. *Trace every query* with per-stage spans and the retrieved ids. Nothing else
  works without it.
. *Log one structured line per query* with `trace_id`, `session_id`,
  `user_id` and the final chunk ids.
. *Stage latency and cost metrics* — cheap, and they make the funnel visible.
. *Refusal rate and zero-results rate* — the two cheapest quality proxies.
. *Citation verification*, emitted as a metric — this becomes your groundedness SLO.
. *Reconciliation job* with alerts on missing, stale and orphaned.
. *Forced sampling* on low-score and failed traces.
. *Feedback capture* (thumbs, copy, follow-up rate) wired back into the
  xref:RAG_Evaluation.adoc[eval set].

Steps 1 and 2 cost about a day and pay for themselves on the first support
ticket. Step 8 is what makes the system improve rather than merely stay
observable.
